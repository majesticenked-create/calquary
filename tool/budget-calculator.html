Frequently asked questions

What is the 50/30/20 budgeting rule?

A guideline that splits after-tax income into roughly 50% needs (housing, food, utilities), 30% wants (discretionary spending), and 20% savings or extra debt payoff - popularized as a simple starting framework, not a strict formula.

What counts as a 'need' versus a 'want'?

Needs are costs you can't avoid without real hardship - rent/mortgage, groceries, utilities, minimum debt payments. Wants are discretionary - dining out, entertainment, subscriptions. The line isn't always sharp, but the split is meant to be a rough guide, not an exact accounting standard.

What if my expenses add up to more than my income?

The calculator shows you're over budget by the shortfall amount - that's a signal to either reduce spending in a category, find additional income, or both, before it compounds into debt.

Should my income here be gross or after-tax?

Use after-tax (take-home) income - it reflects money actually available to spend or save, which is what a monthly budget is built around.
