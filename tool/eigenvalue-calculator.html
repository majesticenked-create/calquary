Frequently asked questions

How do I find eigenvalues of a 2x2 matrix?

Solve det(A − λI) = 0, which for a 2x2 matrix [[a,b],[c,d]] simplifies to λ² − (a+d)λ + (ad−bc) = 0 - a quadratic equation in λ, using the matrix's trace (a+d) and determinant (ad−bc). This calculator applies that formula directly.

How do I find the eigenvector once I have an eigenvalue?

Solve (A − λI)v = 0 for the vector v - for a 2x2 matrix this reduces to one independent equation, since the system is singular by construction. This calculator solves that directly from the b or c entry and the eigenvalue.

What does it mean if the eigenvalues are complex?

Complex eigenvalues mean the matrix has no real eigenvectors - geometrically, this happens for matrices that rotate vectors (like a rotation matrix) rather than just stretching them along fixed directions, since a rotation has no direction that stays unchanged (except a 180° rotation).
