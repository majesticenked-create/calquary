Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between a permutation and a combination?

Permutations count arrangements where order matters (like a race's 1st/2nd/3rd place); combinations count selections where order doesn't matter (like picking a 3-person committee). The same group of items counted as a permutation is always larger, since it counts each ordering separately.

What is 10 choose 3 (10C3)?

120 - using nCr = n! / (r! × (n-r)!): 10! / (3! × 7!) = 3,628,800 / (6 × 5,040) = 120.

What is 10P3?

720 - using nPr = n! / (n-r)!: 10! / 7! = 10 × 9 × 8 = 720.

Why is nPr always nCr multiplied by r!?

Every combination of r items can be arranged in r! different orders, and each of those orderings counts as a separate permutation - so permutations = combinations × r!, or equivalently combinations = permutations ÷ r!.
