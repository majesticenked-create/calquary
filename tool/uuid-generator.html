Frequently asked questions

What is a UUID?

A Universally Unique Identifier - a 128-bit value formatted as 32 hexadecimal digits in five groups (like 8-4-4-4-12 characters), designed so that generating one independently, anywhere, is astronomically unlikely to collide with any other UUID ever generated.

What does 'version 4' mean for a UUID?

Version 4 UUIDs are generated from random (or pseudo-random) bits, as opposed to other UUID versions based on timestamps or namespaces - it's the most common type for general-purpose unique IDs, and what this generator produces.

How likely is a UUID collision?

Effectively negligible - with 122 random bits (a few bits are fixed to mark the version), you'd need to generate roughly a billion UUIDs per second for about 85 years before a 50% chance of any collision, which is why UUIDs are trusted as unique identifiers without checking a central registry.
