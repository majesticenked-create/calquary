Bird Age Calculator

Frequently asked questions

Why doesn't this calculator give a single bird-to-human-year conversion?

Because bird lifespans vary enormously by species - a budgie typically lives 5-10 years while a large macaw can live 50-80 years - so a single universal multiplier would be meaningless across that range. This tool instead groups birds by typical size/lifespan category and estimates a life stage, which is more honest than a fabricated precise conversion.

How accurate are these life-stage age ranges?

They're general estimates based on typical maturity and longevity patterns for each species group, not a scientific study of every species within that group. Individual birds, and specific species within a group, can mature and age at somewhat different rates - treat the result as a rough guide, not an exact classification.

Why do giant parrots have such a long 'adult' stage compared to small birds?

Larger parrot species generally have substantially longer lifespans than small birds - macaws and cockatoos can live 50 years or more in captivity, compared to 5-10 years for a budgie - so their maturity and aging timeline is stretched out proportionally longer.

My bird's species isn't listed exactly - which group should I pick?

Choose the group whose typical size and lifespan most closely matches your bird's species - for example, a Quaker parrot fits reasonably well in the medium group, while a Senegal parrot is closer to the large parrot group. When in doubt, research your specific species' typical lifespan and pick the closest match.

Does sex affect a bird's aging or lifespan?

It can, in some species, though the effect is generally smaller than the effect of species/size group. This calculator doesn't account for sex-based differences, since they vary by species and aren't consistent enough across groups to build into a simple estimate.

Is a 'senior' bird automatically less healthy?

Not necessarily - the senior life stage here just reflects a bird being in the later portion of its typical lifespan for its species group, not a health assessment. Many birds remain healthy well into their senior years with appropriate care; a vet visit is the right way to assess actual health status regardless of age.
