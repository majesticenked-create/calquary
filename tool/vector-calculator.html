Frequently asked questions

What does the dot product tell you?

A single number summarizing how much two vectors point in the same direction, scaled by their lengths - it's positive if the vectors point roughly the same way, negative if roughly opposite, and zero if they're perpendicular.

What does the cross product tell you?

A new vector perpendicular to both input vectors (only defined in 3D), whose magnitude equals the area of the parallelogram formed by the two vectors - commonly used to find a normal vector to a plane or to calculate torque and angular momentum in physics.

How do I find the angle between two vectors?

cos(θ) = (A·B) / (|A|×|B|), then take the inverse cosine (arccos) of that value to get the angle in degrees.

How do I use this for 2D vectors?

Set both z components to 0 - the dot product and angle calculations work identically, and the cross product will only have a z component, which represents the 2D "cross product" (a scalar in most 2D contexts).
