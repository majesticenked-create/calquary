Frequently asked questions

How do I convert Fahrenheit to Celsius?

Subtract 32, then multiply by 5/9. 98.6°F: (98.6 − 32) × 5/9 = 37°C - normal human body temperature.

Why does temperature conversion need an offset, not just multiplication?

Fahrenheit and Celsius have different zero points (freezing water is 0°C but 32°F), so converting requires shifting the scale first, unlike length or weight conversions which only need a multiplier.

What temperature is the same number in both Celsius and Fahrenheit?

−40 degrees - it's the one point where the two scales intersect, since −40°C × 9/5 + 32 = −40°F exactly; every other temperature reads as a different number on each scale.

Does this also convert to and from Kelvin?

Yes - alongside Celsius and Fahrenheit, this converter supports Kelvin, which is useful for scientific calculations since it starts at absolute zero rather than an arbitrary reference point.

What is 180°C in an oven, in Fahrenheit?

356°F exactly, though recipes almost always round oven temperatures to the nearest common setting - 180°C is the standard rounded equivalent of 350°F, a very common baking temperature, even though the precise conversion is a few degrees higher.

What is 100°F in Celsius?

About 37.8°C - subtract 32, then multiply by 5/9: (100−32)×5/9 = 68×5/9 ≈ 37.78°C. That's just above normal human body temperature (37°C), which is why 100°F often feels notably hot outdoors.
