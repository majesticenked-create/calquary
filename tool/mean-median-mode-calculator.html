Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between mean, median, and mode?

Mean is the arithmetic average (sum ÷ count). Median is the middle value when sorted. Mode is the most frequently occurring value. They can all differ, especially with skewed data or outliers.

Why would I use median instead of mean?

Median is more resistant to outliers - a single extreme value can pull the mean far from where most of the data actually sits, while the median stays anchored to the middle of the sorted data regardless of how extreme the outliers are.

Can a data set have more than one mode?

Yes - if two or more values tie for the highest frequency, the data set is "multimodal" and all of them are reported as modes. If every value appears exactly once, there's no meaningful mode.

What does range tell you that mean or median doesn't?

Range measures the spread of the data (maximum minus minimum), while mean and median describe its center - two data sets can have identical means but very different ranges, reflecting very different amounts of variability.
